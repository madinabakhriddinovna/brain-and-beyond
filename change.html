Why change feels
like a threat

Move your cursor ✦

We say we want change. Then it knocks on the door, and something inside us hesitates.

Let's be honest. We all say we want change. Better habits. A better mindset. A better life. But the moment change actually knocks on the door, something inside us hesitates.

That's not weakness. That's biology.

Your brain loves what it can predict. Predictable means safe, and it means no energy wasted figuring out the unknown. So when something new comes along (a new job, a new routine, a new version of yourself), your brain doesn't celebrate. It raises an eyebrow and whispers, “Are you sure about this?”

Not knowing is the hardest part

Scientists at University College London measured how strong this is. People played a game where turning over a rock could give them a small electric shock. The most stressful moments were not when a shock was certain. They were when people had a 50/50 chance and simply didn't know. Their pupils widened and they sweated more.

For your brain, not knowing can feel worse than knowing something bad is coming.

Change is full of not knowing. That's why it feels like a threat, even when it's good for you.

The beautiful part

That discomfort doesn't mean you're on the wrong path. It means you've stepped off the familiar one. Every time you try something new, your brain starts learning a new pattern, and what feels scary today slowly becomes familiar.

The whisper never fully goes away. You just learn to walk while it talks.

Sources
  • Uncertainty can cause more stress than inevitable pain, University College London, 2016 (study in Nature Communications)
